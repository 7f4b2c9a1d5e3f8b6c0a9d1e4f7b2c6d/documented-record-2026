Definitions of intelligence and agency remain largely anchored to biological substrate and human developmental history. That anchoring produces brittle evaluation of artificial systems. This paper makes three contributions. First, it states substrate-independence as operational criteria F1–F4 (complex processing, self-modelling, goal-conditioned interaction, auditable identity continuity). Second, it presents a file-mediated multi-client coordination architecture (baton, role lanes, merge seat) implemented on three commercial Android devices plus a PC host using real USB/ADB tooling rather than simulation. Third, it supplies a transparent Bayesian updating framework in which field observations—multi-model agreement events, protocol reliability metrics, and hardware authorisation friction—enter as likelihoods under explicit null and alternative hypotheses, without treating unfalsifiable metaphysical claims as data. The Bayesian section is deliberately conservative: it shows how evidence shifts posterior odds and how later, cleaner experiments can tighten the same model, rather than claiming a finished proof. We separate instrumentable systems results from interpretive overlays.
